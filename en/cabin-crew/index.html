Three rules for your next trip

A taste of what's inside.

Your night is your home night

Midnight to 6:00 home time is your biological night, wherever you are. If you eat during those hours, keep it light: broth, eggs, yoghurt, cooked vegetables, rice or potato.

On short layovers, don't adjust

On layovers under 48 hours, sleep as close as you can to your home night and eat on home time. That makes getting back home much easier.

Caffeine, a little at a time

About 40 mg every 2 hours in the first half of your duty works better than several coffees in a row at the end of the flight. And none in the 6 hours before you sleep.

Questions

Is the guide free?+

Yes. It's a 14-page PDF. Leave your email above and I'll send it straight over.

Is it for pilots too?+

Yes. Jet lag, eating at odd hours and sleeping on layovers are the same in the cabin and on the flight deck.

What if I fly short-haul?+

The rules on sleep and eating at odd hours still apply, especially with early reports and night flights. The jet lag section is written for long-haul.

Can I take melatonin?+

Many airlines don't allow it for crew, so check your company's policy. The guide explains how to help your body make its own with light, food and a few supplements.

What's a consultation like?+

It's online, by video call, in English or Spanish. We go through your history, your symptoms, your roster and your blood tests, and you get your protocol and your meal plan in an app.
