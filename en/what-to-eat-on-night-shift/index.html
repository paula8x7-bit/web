What to eat on night shift

Before, during and after. With an eating schedule and meal prep ideas that fit in a lunchbox.

I'm a nutritionist and a long haul Inflight Manager, so I spend a lot of nights working. I know what it's like to be starving at 3am and not know whether to eat or hold on until morning.

Here is what the research says about eating at night and how I put it into practice. It works the same whether you're on a ward, in A&E, in a control room or in a warehouse.

By Paula Rubio, integrative nutritionist · November 2026

Why nights are different

Your body runs on an internal clock that expects food during the day and rest at night. Even when you're awake and busy, inside it's still night.

A Harvard team simulated two weeks of night work. People who ate at night got worse at handling glucose; people on the same shifts who kept their meals to daytime hours didn't.1 An Australian team saw the same thing,2 and also found that eating in the early hours raises cortisol.3

You don't have to fast all night. Move the big meal to the daytime and keep the night for something small.

A night shift eating schedule

This is the schedule I'd use for a typical 20:00 to 08:00 night. If your shift starts earlier or later, move everything by the same amount.

For a 20:00 to 08:00 shift

  • 17:00 to 18:00: your main meal, at home.
  • Around midnight: a light snack.
  • 02:00 to 04:00: something small only if you're really hungry, plus water.
  • After 08:00: a small breakfast, then bed.
  • When you wake up: a normal meal, as if it were lunch.

Before your shift: the big meal

Have your main meal before you leave for work, at the time someone on a normal day would have an early dinner.

Make it a full plate: protein (fish, eggs, meat, beans, lentils or tofu), plenty of vegetables, some carbs (rice, potatoes, sweet potato, pasta or bread) and good fat, like olive oil or avocado. That's what keeps you going through the first half of the night.

Ideas for that meal

  • Baked salmon with new potatoes and green beans.
  • Chicken curry with rice and broccoli.
  • A jacket potato with tuna, sweetcorn and a big salad.
  • Lentil and vegetable stew with a boiled egg.

During your shift: keep it light

A team at the University of South Australia compared three options over four nights in a row: a full meal at midnight, a small snack or nothing at all. At 4am, the snack group performed better than the other two, and the full meal group felt sleepier.4

So, around the middle of your shift, have something light and easy to digest. The closer you get to dawn, the smaller it should be.

Keep drinking water through the night. Tiredness and thirst feel very similar, and often what your body is asking for is water.

Night shift meal prep: what I pack

At 3am nobody wants to queue for the microwave or choose between the vending machine and nothing. If it's already in your bag, it's much easier.

I cook once or twice on my days off and portion it into containers: one for the meal before the shift and something small for the night.

In my lunchbox

  • A flask of soup or broth.
  • Natural yoghurt or kefir with fruit.
  • Boiled eggs with cherry tomatoes.
  • Hummus with carrot and cucumber sticks.
  • A handful of nuts.
  • A little rice or potato with vegetables, if it's a long night.

Caffeine in the first half

Caffeine helps in the first half of the shift, but it takes hours to clear. In one study, having it even 6 hours before bed cut sleep short.5

My rule: coffee or tea in the first half of the night, in small amounts, and nothing in the 6 hours before you go to sleep.

After your shift: a light breakfast, then sleep

If you get home hungry, have something small so it doesn't wake you mid morning: a yoghurt, toast with olive oil, a glass of milk or a plant drink. Save the proper meal for when you get up.

No alcohol to help you sleep. It helps you drop off, but it breaks up the second half of the night: more waking and less REM sleep.6 Even small amounts mean your body recovers less while you sleep.7

Night shift and weight gain

A meta-analysis of 28 studies found that people who work nights have about a 23% higher risk of overweight or obesity, and the risk was higher in permanent night workers than in those on rotating shifts.8 In nurses, the link was small overall and showed up mainly in those working nights only.9

Short sleep and stress play a part. So does eating at hours when your body handles food worse.1 Timing is the part you can change tomorrow: the big meal before the shift, something light during it and a small breakfast after.

Your days off

On your days off, go back to eating at daytime hours and use them to shop and prep for the next block of nights. Reviews on meal timing for shift workers agree: eat less at night and be choosy about what you eat.10

Frequently asked questions

Does working night shifts make you gain weight?+

Night work is linked to a higher risk of overweight, especially on permanent nights. Part of it is eating at hours when your body handles food worse. Having your main meal before the shift and only something light in the early hours helps.

Is it better not to eat at all on a night shift?+

Not necessarily. In the Australian study, people who had a small snack performed better in the early hours than those who ate nothing. Something light in the middle of the shift is usually the best middle ground.

What should I eat at 3am on a night shift?+

Something small and easy to digest: a yoghurt, a flask of soup, a boiled egg or a few nuts. Keep bigger meals for before and after your shift.

What's a good night shift eating schedule?+

For a 20:00 to 08:00 shift: main meal at 17:00 or 18:00, a light snack around midnight, only something small after 2am if you need it, and a small breakfast before bed.

When should I stop caffeine on a night shift?+

Have it in the first half of your shift and leave at least 6 hours without caffeine before you go to sleep.
